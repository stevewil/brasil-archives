# Nordeste (Northeast Brazil) Digital Archives Survey — Pipeline Triage

**Purpose:** identify candidates for Mipibu-style treatment (scanned primary sources → structured, searchable, bilingual PT/EN corpus on Flask + SQLite).
**Scope:** RN, PB, PE, AL, SE, BA, CE, PI, MA. Survey date: 24 August 2026.
**Exclusions applied:** Hemeroteca Digital Brasileira, BNDigital, SIAN/Arquivo Nacional, CPDOC/FGV, BDTD, and other large national aggregators.

**Reading the scores:** Mipibu-fit 5 = scanned primary sources, tractable size, individually addressable or enumerable URLs, open access, not already keyword-indexed. 1 = marginal.

## Cross-cutting findings

**1. The Nordeste digital-archive landscape is bimodal.** State public archives are almost uniformly Table 2 material — APERN, APEPB, APA, APEC and (textually) APES publish finding aids at best. The digitally productive institutions are *federal universities* (UFRN/BCZM and LABIM, UFS/SIBIUFS, UFPI/Nupem, UFAL/CPDHis) and *state judiciaries* (TJMA above all). Any pipeline strategy should target universities and courts first.

**2. Scans exist far ahead of publication, and the gap is the opportunity.** The recurring pattern is large image sets already produced but not on the web: APEPI has 450,000+ scans with roughly 5% online ([G1](https://g1.globo.com/pi/piaui/noticia/2025/03/26/arquivo-publico-do-piaui-disponibiliza-apenas-5percent-do-acervo-na-internet-projeto-busca-acelerar-digitalizacao.ghtml)); TJMA has ~2.5 million images with ~10,000 processes accessible ([TJMA](https://www.tjma.jus.br/midia/portal/noticia/517933/dia-internacional-do-arquivo-tribunais-atuam-na-preservacao-da-memoria-da-justica-brasileira)); CPDHis/UFAL holds over 1 TB accessible only in-loco ([CPDHis Guia Geral](https://ichca.ufal.br/pt-br/pesquisa/grupos/historia/centro-de-pesquisa-e-documentacao-historica-cpdhis/guia-geral-do-cpdhis-2024-2.pdf)); UFPB/TJPB produced 151,144 images awaiting internet publication ([CNJ](https://www.cnj.jus.br/acervo-digital-reune-documentos-historicos-do-judiciario-paraibano/)). These institutions need a publication platform, which is precisely what the Mipibu pipeline is.

**3. Three targets are immediately actionable with no negotiation required.** BCZM/UFRN's open directory of ~53,000 newspaper page PDFs (row 3), Jornais de Sergipe's DSpace with sequential handles (row 4), and the Internet Archive's transcribed Atas da Câmara Municipal de Salvador (row 17). All three are open, enumerable and unindexed or under-indexed.

**4. Bahia's best content is currently unreachable.** EAP703's 306,416 pages of Salvador notary books, 1664–1911 — including slave sale and manumission records — remain offline following the British Library's October 2023 cyber-attack ([EAP703](https://eap.bl.uk/project/EAP703)). Meanwhile APEB's index PDFs for inventários, testamentos, escrituras, cíveis and crime *are* on the Internet Archive (row 18). Structuring those indexes now positions the project to attach images the moment the BL catalogue returns.

**5. FamilySearch is a content abundance wrapped in a licensing problem.** Every Nordeste state except Piauí has a partially-indexed parish image collection there, several exceeding a million images. The practical workaround is the Arquidiocese de Natal precedent: FamilySearch delivered the archdiocese its own copy of 1M+ baptism, confirmation and marriage images for local public consultation ([CNBB Nordeste 2](https://cnbbne2.org.br/family-search-entrega-material-digitalizado-a-arquidiocese-de-natal/)). Pursue diocesan copies rather than FamilySearch itself.

**6. Recommended first three projects.** (a) TJMA's 33 downloadable restored processes, 1747–1972 — inventários, testamentos and freedom suits, the closest content match to Mipibu anywhere in the region. (b) BCZM/UFRN's newspaper directory — the lowest-effort, highest-volume corpus available. (c) Nupem Digital's Fundo Inventários and Fundo Igreja Católica — mostly unpublished material with an explicit open-reuse licence and only filename-level search today.

## Method and limitations

- Every value above was taken from a page fetched during this survey on 24 August 2026. Values that could not be confirmed from a fetched page are marked `n.a.` or `unknown`.
- Three hosts were unreachable from the survey environment and their rows are correspondingly incomplete: APEB's AtoM (`atom.fpc.ba.gov.br`, DNS failure), FUNDAJ's Acervo Digital (`digitalizacao.fundaj.gov.br`, connection failure), and APEJE's AtoM (`acervo.arquivopublico.pe.gov.br`, `disallow_by_robots`). The APEJE robots restriction is a genuine ToS signal, not a transient error.
- FamilySearch's collection browse interface is JavaScript-rendered and returned no extractable content; collection IDs, date ranges and record/image counts were therefore taken from FamilySearch's own wiki pages and from published collection-update trackers, which are cited per row.
- Mipibu-fit scores are the surveyor's judgement based on the cited fields; they are not themselves sourced.
- Institutions excluded by the brief (Hemeroteca Digital Brasileira, BNDigital, SIAN/Arquivo Nacional, CPDOC/FGV, BDTD) are omitted from both tables even where they hold Nordeste material.
